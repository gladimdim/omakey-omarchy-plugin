Fires on touch-down

Ten fingers. Every chord.

Ordinary touch buttons fire when you lift your finger. Omakey fires the moment it lands, and every finger is its own held key.

Hold CTRL SHIFT T with three fingers. Hold Shift with your thumb and type. Hold Fn and the keys show their media legends.

Fast and unbreakable

Lost packets heal. Keys never stick.

Every UDP packet carries the complete set of held keys plus every tap not yet acknowledged. Lose one and the next one fixes it.

If the phone goes quiet for 500 ms while holding keys, the desktop lets go. No runaway Shift, ever.

Slowed down 6×. Real heartbeat: every 100 ms, with resends after 1.5 × the measured ping. Packets are marked DSCP EF, so Wi-Fi queues them like voice calls.

Touchpad

Pull down. It's a trackpad.

Tap the ⌄ touchpad handle and a touchpad slides over the keys: move, tap to click, hold for the menu, two fingers to scroll.

Click buttons run down both sides, mirrored for either thumb: Left, Right, Ctrl + Left and Shift + Left for multi-select. Each computer remembers its own pointer speed.

Security

Anyone who can type on your desktop owns it.

So nobody else can. Omakey talks to your desktop directly, over your own network. Nothing sits in between.

A 256-bit key in the QR code

Good for one phone and five minutes. Both screens show the same fingerprint before anything is saved.

Every packet sealed

AES-256-GCM with fresh HKDF session keys and replay counters. A packet that doesn't verify is dropped silently.

Nothing runs as root

A udev rule gives the logged-in user /dev/uinput. The daemon is a sandboxed systemd user service.

Private networks only

Firewall rules let in home, office and Tailscale addresses, with a handshake rate limit per address.

Keys stay on the phone

Pairing keys are encrypted with an Android Keystore key that can't be exported. Backups and device transfer are off.

No accounts, no cloud

Nothing to sign up for, no telemetry. Open source under MIT, with a published wire protocol and test vectors.

For tinkerers

A daemon you can script.

Everything the widget does is a command: pair, status, devices, forget, rename, config. A dry-run mode prints keys instead of typing, and a test client acts as a phone.

The wire protocol, layout format and cross-language test vectors are documented, so you can write your own client.

Omarchy plugin 1.2.1

The omakeyd service and the bar widget. Needs the Omarchy shell and Rust (sudo pacman -S rust).

omarchy plugin add https://github.com/gladimdim/omakey-omarchy-plugin --enable
~/.config/omarchy/plugins/gladimdim.omakey/install.sh
  1. Run both. install.sh builds the daemon and asks for sudo once (udev rule, firewall).
  2. Click the keyboard icon in the bar → Pair a phone.
  3. Scan it with the app. Type.
Or from a terminal: omakeyd pair. Uninstall: install.sh --uninstall.
